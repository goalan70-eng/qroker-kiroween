## Narration (approximately 240 words)

QRoker turns card placement into a shared prediction puzzle. This is the actual browser Practice Mode, using fifty-two cards on an eight-by-eight board. I choose English, one human, three local AI opponents, and a reproducible seed.

I drag a card from my Hand onto a legal cell. The preview lets me inspect the move before I confirm it. Clubs and spades can rotate when that changes their corner arrangement. There are no jokers or Swap actions.

When a placement completes a valid group, each player predicts its hidden total. I submit my Guess, then the public reveal shows the cards, total, and points. Other players' private Guesses stay hidden. Current scoring is provisional, including ten points for an exact Guess.

I reload and choose Continue. The saved game restores the Board and turn. After more real turns, shown here with a labelled cut, the game reaches its final ranking. The AI runs locally without a paid model API.

These are repository artifacts, not a recorded Kiro session. The owner reports primary spec-driven Kiro development. The requirements, design, tasks, and hook configurations are preserved here. Later Codex audits, score changes, optical repairs, and these submission materials are attributed separately. Original Kiro sessions were not audited.

The optical prototype remains separate. Synthetic improvements do not establish phone-camera accuracy. Printed-card trials, camera integration, and physical privacy checks are still pending. Today's demonstration is the browser game.
