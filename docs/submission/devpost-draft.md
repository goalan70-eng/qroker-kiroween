## Inspiration

What if arranging ordinary playing cards created both a strategy puzzle and a visual code? QRoker explores that idea through a playable browser game and a separate optical research prototype. Players choose where to place a card, watch for new two-by-two patterns, and predict a hidden total before the public reveal.

## What it does

Practice Mode uses a standard 52-card deck on an eight-by-eight board, without jokers or a Swap action. One human can play against three local AI opponents; local hot-seat play is also supported. Drag a card to preview its position, then confirm the move. A newly completed valid group triggers private Guesses followed by a public reveal, points and continued play toward the final ranking. English and Korean UI, deterministic seeds and local save/resume make the game reproducible and easy to revisit.

The AI uses local game strategies and requires no paid model API. Its input is a player-specific view rather than the complete hidden state. New-game scoring is provisional: an exact Guess earns 10, errors one through five earn 5/4/3/2/1, and emptying a Hand adds 5. These values have not been established as balanced; old saves retain their saved score configuration.

## Challenges and lessons

Hidden information is a system boundary. UI and AI consume a restricted player view, while saves replay actions with their stored configuration. Acceptance testing also revealed why task labels alone cannot establish implementation: the provenance audit recorded missing evidence instead of inferring it from commit subjects.

Optical experiments taught a separate lesson: passing synthetic tests does not establish camera reliability. The recent bounded experiment improved independent near-45-degree synthetic positives from 225/252 to 252/252, while retaining selected negative controls. It is not a camera accuracy result or a general robustness rate. Default radius 1 still incorrectly accepts ten deliberately selected four-bit corruption counterexamples. No printer/phone trial was available, and physical privacy and registration remain unvalidated.

## What works today, and what comes next

The demonstrated product is the browser Practice Mode. The optical decoder, frozen marker codebook and print/photo preparation tools remain independent research artifacts; camera decoding in the browser and Scoring Companion integration are unimplemented. The game enforces fragment-corner validity in its rules; visual schematic code overlays remain deferred.

Next steps are owner review of the stacked PRs, verification of the final integrated revision, score-balance experiments, physical print/camera trials and later browser-safe optical integration. Public deployment and video publication require explicit authorization. This draft makes no completed physical-game, real-phone performance or final contest-eligibility claim.
